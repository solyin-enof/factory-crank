# Lessons

- A control hint at the bottom is not adequate onboarding for an unfamiliar gesture. Introduce the player's goal before play, make the next action prominent, and provide an obvious accessible alternative. Clicking or holding a large machine control must give immediate visible feedback.
- Machine gestures must express the machine's work. A generic hold shortcut erases the physical interaction and makes this game feel like an idle clicker. Show the crank driving the furnace blower, and use a lever connected to the press ram. Teach through visible mechanism and gesture demonstration.
- Audio level needs live playtest feedback; synthesized effects that are audible in isolation can be too quiet during play. Raise machinery and impact levels when requested.
- Teach this game by introducing machinery gradually. Begin with only ore, furnace and blower; reveal the press when two plates exist, routing after the first gear, and the motor near its milestone. Do not solve a crowded first screen by adding a mandatory tutorial.
- Browser gesture surfaces must disable text selection and touch callouts on the control and its children, prevent native drag/context menus, and capture each pointer. Verify game input does not trigger copy/paste selection on mobile.
- For mobile development through a named LAN/VPN host, verify the actual Host header against the live server. Binding to 0.0.0.0 does not imply that Vite permits a custom hostname; explicitly allow the intended hostname.
